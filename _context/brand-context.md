## What it is

Woodstock Cabinet Makers is a well-established, 40+ year custom cabinetry business serving Brisbane homeowners. Core offering is whole-home custom joinery, not just kitchens:

1. **Kitchens** — the flagship category, premium/design-led.
2. **Laundry** — custom laundry cabinetry and storage.
3. **Walk-in wardrobes / bedroom storage.**
4. **Garage storage** — a newer/underused promotional angle worth pushing (see Priorities).
5. Website also lists bathroom and home/work office cabinetry as categories.

This is the opposite situation from Bespoke Woodcraft: Woodstock is not a new or unproven brand. It has real tenure, real completed projects, and real word-of-mouth reputation (see Competitive context). The problem is not trust — it's visibility. The business has almost no active online presence despite 40 years of runs on the board.

## Growth stage

Established, not new. 40+ years in business, existing client base, existing reviews and referral network. But the digital/social presence has been essentially frozen for years — Instagram's pinned content and most engagement trace back to 2017–2018, Facebook shows minimal recent activity. This is a **revival**, not a launch: the job is to bring an already-credible business into an active, consistent online presence, not to build credibility from zero.

## Priorities this quarter

1. **Audit existing Facebook and Instagram** — understand what's there, what's stale, what needs fixing (profile info, cover photo, bio, pinned content) before posting anything new.
2. **Start posting consistently** — the account has followers and history but no current activity. Re-establishing a regular cadence is the immediate priority.
3. Push garage storage as a category — it's on the services list but underrepresented in existing content, and (like Bespoke Woodcraft's garage cabinets pillar) is likely to be a strong top-of-funnel, broadly shareable content angle.

## Competitive context

Brisbane market, but Woodstock competes at the premium/high-end tier of custom cabinetry, not against big-box (IKEA, Bunnings-style flat-pack) options. The existing brand voice and visual identity (see brand-voice.md) already position Woodstock alongside high-end interior design and renovation businesses. Real, specific differentiator vs. competitors: 40 years of tenure, an approachable and hands-on owner (Jack), and genuine client reviews praising trustworthiness, problem-solving, and craftsmanship (see ideal-customer-profile.md).

One known weak point from public reviews: at least one complaint about wood matching/fit issues on a job. Not a pattern, but worth being mindful of — don't overclaim flawless execution; let real finished work and real reviews do the talking instead.

## Where the audience is

1. **Word of mouth / referral** — historically the strongest channel for this business, per existing five-star reviews describing repeat engagements and recommendations.
2. **Google / local search** — "custom cabinet maker Brisbane," "kitchen renovation Archerfield" type searches from homeowners actively planning a project.
3. **Instagram and Facebook** — both have an existing (small but real) follower base and old content with genuine engagement, but need to be revived. Given the premium positioning, Instagram is likely to carry more long-term weight here than it did for Bespoke Woodcraft.

## Rules for handling marketing tasks for this brand

1. This is a different business from Bespoke Woodcraft, same industry — don't reuse or mix content, captions, or context between the two. Treat every task for Woodstock independently.
2. Lead with real tenure and real completed work (40 years, genuine reviews, Jack as the face of the business) — unlike Bespoke Woodcraft, overclaiming here is not a risk; underusing this asset is.
3. Keep the premium/design-led positioning the brand already has (see brand-voice.md) rather than flattening it to a plain, budget-conscious tone.
4. Every homeowner-facing category (kitchens, wardrobes, laundry, garage storage) should feel like part of the same premium, considered-design identity — don't let garage storage content feel like a cheaper, bolted-on add-on.
5. Because the social accounts have been dormant for years, early content should acknowledge freshness/activity implicitly (recent real projects, current dates, "here's what we're building now") rather than pretending there's been no gap.
6. Favor real project photos and specifics (materials, room type, what made it tricky) over generic luxury adjectives with nothing behind them.
