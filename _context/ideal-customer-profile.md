# Ideal Customer Profile — Woodstock Cabinet Makers

## Primary persona: The Established Brisbane Homeowner Investing in Design

- Homeowner (not renter) renovating an established home, or building/fitting out a new high-end home.
- Likely mid-30s to 60s+, higher income bracket than the typical mass-market renovator — this persona is closer to the top of the market than Bespoke Woodcraft's audience.
- Brisbane-based, with a lean toward inner and southside suburbs given the Archerfield location, though the brand's premium positioning can travel further across the city.

### Psychographics

- Sees their home as an extension of their lifestyle and taste, not just a functional space — buys into language like "spaces you love to live in," not just "storage."
- Wants design that's personalised and considered, not off-the-shelf — values the conversation and process (Woodstock's site literally frames this: "asking the question, what unique elements do we need to consider").
- Comfortable paying a premium for quality and craftsmanship; not the price-sensitive, IKEA-comparison shopper Bespoke Woodcraft targets.
- Trusts referrals and real reputation over ads — this persona is more likely to arrive via word of mouth or search after hearing the name than through a viral cold reach.

### Buying triggers

- A home renovation (kitchen, bathroom, laundry, wardrobe) on an established or higher-value home.
- Building or fitting out a new home and wanting joinery to match the quality of the build.
- Garage storage as a lower-commitment, easier entry point — someone who isn't ready for a full kitchen renovation yet but wants a taste of Woodstock's quality first.

### Objections — different from Bespoke Woodcraft

This persona does **not** need convincing that custom is worth it over big-box — they're already past that. The real objections are:

- **"Are they still around / still good?"** — the business has 40 years of reputation but a dormant online presence. A homeowner who gets referred to Woodstock and then checks Instagram/Facebook and sees nothing since 2017–2018 may wonder if the business is still active or has kept up with modern standards. Fresh, current content directly solves this.
- **Execution risk on the details** — at least one public review flagged fit/matching issues on a job. Not a dominant pattern, but this persona (who's paying a premium) cares about precision and finishing, so content that shows attention to detail (measuring, fitting, finishing) reassures rather than just showing pretty finished shots.
- **Communication and process** — reviews consistently praise Jack as approachable and a good listener; leaning into that personal, hands-on relationship (vs. a faceless large renovation company) is a real differentiator.

### Where they spend time online

1. **Word of mouth / referral** — still likely the number one channel; existing reviews describe repeat clients and direct recommendations.
2. **Google / local search** — used to verify a referral or find a cabinet maker while actively planning a renovation.
3. **Instagram** — for this premium persona, Instagram carries real weight as a portfolio and "is this brand current" check, more so than it did for Bespoke Woodcraft's audience.
4. **Facebook** — secondary but still relevant, especially for slightly older segments of this persona.

### What actually changes marketing approach

- Don't relitigate the "why pay more than big-box" argument — that's not this audience's objection. Lead with design, personalisation, and finished quality instead.
- Prioritise proving the business is active and current today (recency, real dated projects) over proving it's trustworthy in the abstract — trust already exists via reputation, it just needs to be visible.
- Garage storage content can pull in a slightly broader/less exclusively premium audience as a secondary, lower-commitment entry point — similar role to how Bespoke Woodcraft is using garage cabinets as a top-of-funnel play, but here it's introducing an already-trusted premium brand to new categories rather than building trust from scratch.
